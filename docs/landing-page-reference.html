The two ideas everything is built on

Two spines. Every module hangs off them.

SPINE 01

Nothing is assigned to a company.

It is assigned to a discipline, and companies hold disciplines. Novate the architect and every drawing, every warranty, every scope line follows — because nothing was ever pinned to the old company in the first place. And the gap the matrix shows is the gap the warranty register shows, because they are the same lookup.

SPINE 02

No date is ever typed.

Every date is a programme line plus an offset. Re-import the programme and the whole project reschedules: drawings, instalments, conditions, credits, change-request decisions. Slip the Stage 4 freeze and you see four payment instalments move with it — a cashflow consequence of a design delay that nobody usually connects to the delay itself.

What it does

Three tiers. One codebase. Switch modules on per project.

Why it is different

It infers. It does not count.

Derived, never stored

Construction status, due dates, overdue counts, risk exposure, whether a change may proceed — computed on read, every time. Anything stored that could disagree with reality eventually will, silently. This never does.

Hi-vis means one thing

An unallocated gap. Not overdue, not urgent, not a warning — a thing nobody has been given. It is the colour a design manager scans for on a Monday, and it is never spent on anything else.

Consultants use it across contractors

One login, however many main contractors a consultant works for. Each contractor sees only their own projects. The consultant sees their own front on every job — and never sees that the other contractor exists.
